Research question

How can I use AI to develop a medieval-inspired Metroidvania where the characters, environments, and gameplay work together while staying true to my creative vision?

Why it matters

I want to turn my ideas into a game world that feels personal and connected. AI could help me explore concepts and work through technical challenges, but I want to understand how much direction and revision it takes to keep the results original. This project gives me a chance to explore how I can use AI while making the creative decisions myself.

Audience and context

This project is for players who enjoy medieval settings, exploration, environmental storytelling, and Metroidvania games. For my Applied AI course, I’m investigating how AI can support the creative process while I make the decisions about the game’s identity, story, and visual direction.

What I need to learn

  • How to communicate my creative vision through prompts, visual references, and clear constraints.
  • How to develop characters, enemies, and environments that feel like they belong in the same world.
  • How to connect exploration, combat, and unlockable abilities in a small Metroidvania level.
  • How to reveal a story through locations, encounters, and discoveries.
  • How to evaluate AI’s suggestions and revise results that feel generic or disconnected.
  • What I can realistically build and test within the project’s timeframe.

Possible outcomes

  • A small playable level

    A prototype with connected areas, basic combat, and an unlockable ability that lets players reach a previously inaccessible path.

  • A visual worldbuilding collection

    Character, enemy, and environment concepts, supported by a map and notes explaining how they connect.

  • An interactive story prototype

    A short exploration experience where discoveries and revisiting locations gradually reveal the world’s history.

Sources and notes

The World Design of Hollow Knight | Boss Keys

Mark Brown / Game Maker’s Toolkit · Video essay

Examines how Hollow Knight’s world is structured for exploration. It could help me plan connected areas, shortcuts, and progression so my medieval Metroidvania feels connected and gives players reasons to revisit locations.

Your First 2D Game

Godot Engine documentation contributors · Official game-development tutorial

Covers building a small 2D game, including organizing a project, moving a character, and creating enemies. It could help me understand the technical basics and judge what I can realistically include in a playable prototype.

Evolving Roles and Workflows of Creative Practitioners in the Age of Generative AI

Srishti Palani and Gonzalo Ramos · Research paper, ACM Creativity and Cognition, 2024

Explores how creative practitioners use generative AI, including their roles, workflows, and challenges. It could help me think about where AI fits into my process and how to keep my own judgment and creative vision central as I develop the game.

Game-making tools to evaluate

  • Godot — open-source game engine; the tutorial above uses it.
  • GDevelop — free, open-source, no-code game engine with AI assistance.
  • Rosebud — AI-assisted game creation in the browser.
